Research Overview & Future Vision

My research focuses on multiscale materials characterization, chemical imaging, and durability mechanics to accelerate the design, testing, and deployment of ultra-low-carbon civil infrastructure materials.

THRUST 01

Multi-Modal Phase Quantification of Anhydrous Cements

Confocal Raman Spectroscopy • X-ray Diffraction (XRD) • Clinker Mineralogy • CSA & Blended Binders

Accurately quantifying individual clinker mineral phases (Alite, Belite, Aluminate, Ferrite, Ye'elimite, Anhydrite) in unhydrated cements is essential for predicting setting kinetics, early strength development, and carbon reduction potential. Conventional methods (e.g., XRD Rietveld refinement alone, SEM-EDS) face persistent limitations with amorphous phase blindness, preferred orientation artifacts, and overlapping diffraction peaks.

Core Breakthroughs:

  • Established a combined XRD and 2D/3D Confocal Raman Imaging framework demonstrating strong synergy: XRD accurately quantifies crystalline polymorph fractions, while Raman imaging maps sub-micron spatial distributions and resolves minor phases without requiring destructive chemical extractions.
  • Systematically investigated analysis parameters—demonstrating how preferred orientation models, Rietveld phase addition order, and Raman optical binarization algorithms influence quantitative phase estimates.
  • Successfully applied this methodology to quantify low-carbon Calcium Sulfoaluminate (CSA) cements, resolving ye'elimite and sulfate distribution at the single-particle level.

THRUST 02

UR²: 5-Minute Reactivity Test for SCMs & Calcined Clays

High-Throughput Quality Control • Colorimetric Dissolution • Low-Carbon LC³ Cements

Calcined clays and supplementary cementitious materials (SCMs) are the primary pathway to reducing cement's carbon footprint globally. However, evaluating clay pozzolanic reactivity currently requires the ASTM C1897 standard (the R³ test), which takes 3 to 7 days of isothermal calorimetry or furnace bound-water testing. This multi-day latency creates a critical bottleneck for real-time industrial plant operations.

Core Breakthroughs:

  • Developed the Ultra-Rapid Reactivity (UR²) test: a novel chemical dissolution assay that dissolves clay specimens in 4 M NaOH at 90°C for just 5 minutes, followed by rapid spectrophotometric / colorimetric quantification of dissolved active Si and Al.
  • Validated across 47 diverse clay samples globally: the resulting dissolution index (1.54Al + Si) achieves an outstanding correlation with 7-day R³ heat (R² = 0.92, RMSE = 94.1 J/g) and 28-day mortar compressive strength (R² = 0.94, RMSE = 1.7 MPa).
  • Demonstrated that the UR² test can be implemented with off-the-shelf, low-cost cameras and smartphone colorimetry, enabling instant field screening for cement plants and testing laboratories.
Related Publications:
• Cement and Concrete Research (2025), 10.1016/j.cemconres.2025.107806
• Featured by Illinois News Bureau & CEE at Illinois (February 2025)
THRUST 03

Sustainable Utilization of Quarry By-Products in Pavements

Aggregate Beneficiation • Hydrotalcite Formation • Piezoelectric Wave Sensing • ICT / IDOT Partnership

Quarry by-products (QBs)—fine aggregate residues (<6 mm) generated during stone crushing—accumulate in massive stockpiles across the United States. In research sponsored by the Illinois Center for Transportation (ICT) and Illinois DOT, our team investigated lightly cement-stabilized QB materials as sustainable pavement base and subbase alternatives.

Core Breakthroughs:

  • Discovered the mineralogical origin of superior multi-year strength gain in dolomitic QBs compared to limestone: in situ Raman imaging confirmed that hydrotalcite (Mg₆Al₂CO₃(OH)₁₆•4H₂O) precipitates along dolomite aggregate boundaries, refining pore structures and resisting repetitive freeze-thaw degradation.
  • Implemented piezoelectric Bender Element (BE) sensors transmitting elastic shear waves (Vs) to continuously monitor non-destructive stiffness evolution and curing kinetics in real time under elevated field-curing conditions (40°C).
  • Demonstrated that combining 0.5–0.75% metakaolin with lightly stabilized cement achieves a 1.7–2.6× strength increase, minimizing virgin cement requirements in road geotechnical layers.

THRUST 04

Chemo-Thermal-Hygral Modeling of Concrete Durability & DEF

Delayed Ettringite Formation • Internal Sulfate Attack • Heat Curing Kinetics • University of Tokyo

Delayed Ettringite Formation (DEF) is a destructive concrete degradation mechanism occurring in steam-cured precast concrete and mass concrete structures exposed to high early-age hydration temperatures (>65–70°C). High temperatures destabilize primary ettringite, trapping sulfate and aluminate in C-S-H gel; upon subsequent moisture exposure, late ettringite crystallization generates severe internal expansion pressures and microcracking.

Core Breakthroughs:

  • Developed an advanced chemo-thermal-hygral numerical simulation model at The University of Tokyo coupling multi-component hydration kinetics, temperature evolution during heat treatment, non-linear moisture diffusion, and sulfate adsorption isotherms.
  • Established quantitative criteria predicting the kinetics and ultimate expansion thresholds as a function of cement alkali content, sulfate/aluminate ratio, maximum curing temperature, and duration of thermal exposure.
Related Publications:

Future Faculty Research Direction

As a prospective faculty member, my laboratory will establish a research program focused on Advanced Micro-Spectroscopy and Resilient, Decarbonized Infrastructure Materials. Core future directions include:

  • In Situ & Operando Raman Micro-Spectroscopy: Real-time mapping of dissolution, precipitation, and carbonation fronts in alternative cementitious binders (magnesium cements, alkali-activated materials, and LC³).
  • AI-Driven High-Throughput Reactivity Screening: Coupling machine vision, automated colorimetry, and spectral deconvolution for autonomous quality verification of non-traditional pozzolans and industrial solid wastes.
  • Physics-Informed Chemo-Mechanical Durability Prediction: Integrating multi-scale microstructural data into finite-element durability models for infrastructure asset management under changing environmental extremes.
